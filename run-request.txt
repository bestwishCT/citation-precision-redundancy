33: 3.4 ALCE is Robust to Shortcut Cases
34: We showcase how the ALCE evaluation is robust
35: to two possible shortcuts in §D: (1) using the top-1
36: retrieved passage as the response and citing itself,
37: and (2) using the first two sentences of the top-1

43: In this section, we discuss three major modeling

41: E Citation Recall Discussion
42: Our citation precision evaluation cannot detect a
43: citation that partially supports the statement and
44: hence will falsely penalize it. Consider a state-
45: ments3 and its citations [2][4][5]: if [2] entails
46: partial information ofs3 that [4][5] also entails,
47: [2] will be counted as “irrelevant” while it should
48: not be penalized. Liu et al. (2023) conduct hu-
49: man evaluation on citation precision in a different
50: way: For each citation, they ask annotators to judge
51: whether the citation (1) fully support, (2) partially
52: support, or (3) does not supportsi. One citationci,j
53: is precise if (a)ci,jfully supportssi or (b)Ci fully
54: supportssi,ci,jpartially supportssi, and noc∈Ci
55: alone fully supportssi. This evaluation solved the
56: corner case we mentioned in the main paper (one
57: citation partially supports the claim but is identi-
58: fied as “irrelevant”). However, it is challenging to
59: conduct such evaluation automatically, as there is
60: no existing model that can judge whether a cita-
61: tion “partially” supports a claim. We also explore
62: prompting ChatGPT to conduct such a task, which
63: yields poor results. We defer it to future work to
64: collect supervised data to train a betterϕthat can
65: detect “partial support”.
